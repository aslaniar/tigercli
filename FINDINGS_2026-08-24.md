candidate, and it is bounded.

window that closed before our first body was ever sent.

follows the wire; the alternatives (suppress the frame, or synthesise an
incremental) both break a contract we have measured.

## 20.38 THE VISIBILITY LAYER DOES NOT EXIST, AND THAT REFRAMES THE WHOLE
## PEER FRONT: THE PEER LINK IS NOT ONE LINK IN THE CHAIN, IT IS THE ENTIRE
## MECHANISM.

Prompted by the user's challenge: "for number 2, you say one boot is all we
need, but you've said that everytime. do a full upstream to downstream
analysis again." Three times I named a single blocker and was wrong. This
pass traced every link instead of guessing at one.

The finding that matters is not another missing field. It is that I have been
carrying a mental model in which the server can, eventually, make one guardian
visible to another. It cannot. Nothing in either tree can.

### What is actually absent (not gated off - absent)

  - `EntityType::playerBroadcast` is DEFINED in
    `middleware/gameplay/external/external_entity_codec.h:39` and NEVER
    CONSTRUCTED. Zero `EntityType::playerBroadcast` sites in the tree.
  - `prepare_external_shadow` and `read_empty_frame`: zero callers.
  - `activation.gameplayExternalBody` and `activation.serverDefaultEntity`:
    zero consumers. The only two activation gates anything reads are
    `activityPublicMembership` (keepalive push) and `physicsHostSession`
    (physics_session.cpp:562).
  - `physicsHostSession`, by upstream's own comment at
    `core/settings/server/activation/definition.h:36-39`: "It produces no wire
    output either way."
  - `server/gameplay` contains no relay and no forward path. The server cannot
    carry peer traffic even in principle.
  - `world_population` (the one entity emitter,
    `activity_message_push.cpp:104`) is off, unvalidated, and aimed at static
    scenario entities, not peers.

### The consequence

Guardian-to-guardian visibility in D2 is peer-authored. Two clients replicate
each other directly over the gameplay UDP protocol once a posse link exists.
The server's entire contribution is matchmaking and descriptor delivery.

So the peer link is not step 6 of 8. It is the whole product. There is no
downstream we own, no fallback, and no server-side workaround. If B never
opens a socket to A, nothing else we can build produces a visible guardian.

### Three corrections this forces

  1. **p2(27) was right, and now provably so.** Serving A's own stored
     descriptor bytes instead of a server-built one is correct - a server-built
     descriptor points B at our endpoint, and our endpoint has nothing behind
     it. I switched without justification (see 20.37); the justification exists.

  2. **`advertised_address` / `transport_address` / port 30976 are dead config
     for this front.** They matter only if we ever host. Currently
     192.168.1.164:30976 in the deployed settings, and irrelevant.

  3. **The physics gates are NOT on the critical path.** I have been carrying
     `physicsHostSession` / `serverDefaultEntity` / `gameplayExternalBody` as
     "things to flip later." Flipping all three changes nothing about whether
     two guardians see each other. They belong to a different capability
     (server-authored entities) that is unfinished in both trees.

### The ladder, honestly marked

  | # | Link                                   | Status |
  |---|----------------------------------------|--------|
  | 1 | A publishes a 128-byte descriptor      | arrival VERIFIED; **contents NEVER INSPECTED** |
  | 2 | B issues svc-43 search                 | VERIFIED (once, at setup:matchmaking) |
  | 3 | We answer in the client's own shape    | VERIFIED (146B, field 3 container, IdPair) |
  | 4 | B parses the answer                    | ASSUMED - "no error" is not "understood" |
  | 5 | B decides to join                      | UNKNOWN - never observed |
  | 6 | B opens UDP to A's endpoint            | **NEVER HAPPENED** - 0 receives, 0 egress denials |
  | 7 | A admits B                             | untested |
  | 8 | The two replicate each other           | not ours at all - pure client-to-client |

### The largest untested assumption is link 1, not link 3

We store A's 128 bytes and forward them blind. We have never parsed one. If A
publishes an endpoint that is not reachable - 0.0.0.0, a loopback, a Wine-side
address, or a descriptor with no public entry (which per `join_descriptor.h`
makes the peer "unroutable" and sends the client to NAT traversal) - then B is
behaving CORRECTLY by doing nothing, and every field we add to the search
result is wasted work.

This is cheap to settle. `descriptor/join_descriptor.cpp` already fixes the
layout: machine id at 0, NetAddr at 8, address at +0 (network order), port at
+4 (memory order), public address at +30, public port at +34, NAT type at +40,
method at +85, session id at 110. A decoder mirrors those offsets. We have a
writer and have never written the reader.

### Method note

Three wrong "one boot away" calls share one cause: I treated the first
plausible missing piece as the blocker and stopped looking. The ladder above
is the corrective - every link gets a mark, and "assumed" is written down as
assumed rather than quietly promoted to verified.

## 20.40 THE PUBLISHED DESCRIPTOR CARRIES NO IP AND NO PORT. IT CARRIES A
## STEAM IDENTITY AND A STEAM LOBBY - AND BOTH OF OURS ARE FABRICATED
## CONSTANTS, IDENTICAL ON EVERY MACHINE.

The instrument that finally ran (p2(32), deployed exe 8605161b5c3c4db6,
provenance asserted) dumped the 128 bytes the client publishes on every
advertisement_update. Liveness: 7 advertisements, 7 publish lines, 14 raw
lines.

### The actual structure

```
[0..7]     machine id, LE u64            0xAB8863EE91D2D108  (varies per boot)
[8..49]    ASCII, 42 bytes               "steamid:76561198776753861#dde342bff358429e"
[50..92]   zeros
[93]       0x06
[94..109]  16-byte key                   01A7BAA447527A4B3A6AEFBA3577F0CF
[110..117] LE u64                        0x0109000000000003
[118..127] zeros
```

**There is no IPv4 address and no port anywhere in it.**

### What that settles

  1. **Link 6 is explained.** Client B never opened a socket to A's endpoint
     because the advertisement never names an endpoint. Zero receives on
     30976 and zero egress denials were never evidence of a transport
     problem - there was never going to be a connection attempt to observe.
     The peer-subnet egress relaxation (p2(28)) was answering a question
     nobody asked.

  2. **Our descriptor model is wrong.** `JoinEndpoint` is {machineId,
     address, port, onlineSessionId} and `write_net_addr` lays out local and
     public IPv4 entries, a NAT type and a method byte. Read against the real
     bytes, our "address" field is the text "stea", our "port" is "mi", our
     "public address" is "42bf", our "NAT type" is '9'. Only offset 0
     (machine id) and offset 110 land on real fields, and the field at 110 is
     a Steam lobby id, not an online session id in the sense we meant.

  3. **The join key we hardcode to zero is populated.** `build()` carries the
     comment "The join key stays zero. The direct path accepts it and carries
     no key material." The client writes 16 bytes there.

  4. **Byte 93 is 0x06.** Our own comment in join_descriptor.cpp says methods
     6 and 7 select the relay rather than the direct path. PROVENANCE NOT
     VERIFIED BY ME - the comment entered in commit 122b8da and I have not
     traced it to a client-side source. Recorded because it is consistent
     with an advertisement that carries no address: a relay method needs
     none. Do not build on it without checking.

### The hard blocker, and it is ours

The published SteamID is `76561198776753861` = `0x0110000130AA9EC5`, which is
`kLocalSteamId` - a HARDCODED CONSTANT in our own Steam shim
(`steam/interfaces/methods/common.cpp:18`), returned by `get_steam_id` to
every caller.

**Every instance of the client reports the same Steam identity.** The Mac and
the rig are, to the game, the same Steam user. Two clients cannot form a peer
session as one identity.

The lobby is the same story: `0x0109000000000003` is
`kLobbySteamIdPrefix | call` from `matchmaking.cpp:54`, a lobby our shim
invents per API call, with nothing behind it and no existence outside the
process that made it up.

### What this means for the front

Peer discovery and transport are addressed by **Steam identity and Steam
lobby**, not by IP. So the whole svc-43 search-result effort - p2(26)'s
encoder, p2(27)'s foreign_advertisement and IdPair, the field-3 lane - was
built to deliver a descriptor whose contents point at a Steam layer that is
stubbed. Delivering it perfectly changes nothing while the identity inside it
is a constant and the lobby is imaginary.

This also removes the mystery from upstream's demo. A working two-player
fireteam needs the Steam layer to work; it does not need a search-result
encoder, which is exactly why their tree answers search with an empty message
and demos it anyway.

### The next move, and its honest limit

Make `kLocalSteamId` per-instance configurable so the two machines stop
sharing one identity. Cheap, obviously correct - two players must not be one
user - and it is the first item that serves goal 1 (separate accounts) and
goals 2/3 at once.

It does NOT make multiplayer work. Distinct identities remove one hard
blocker; whether the client can then broker a real join through our stubbed
lobby layer is untested and probably negative. Marked so it does not become
the fourth "one boot away."
